# Saint of the Day portrait restoration

The Today card now always has a decorative **offline-safe illustrated saint silhouette** to the left of the biography. When an identity-matched sacred artwork has been researched, a source-linked image overlays this fallback. An unavailable network image removes only the overlay; the illustrated silhouette remains visible.

Curated examples (name matching, **never** automatic date or vague image search):
- Saint Clement of Rome: [Saint Paul Outside the Walls medallion](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:4-St.Clement_I.jpg), public domain.
- Saint Catherine of Alexandria (Western and Byzantine): [fourteenth-century icon](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:St.CatherineofAlexandria.jpg), public domain.
- Saint Andrew Dung-Lac: [Westerville parish stained glass photograph](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Saint_Paul_Catholic_Church_(Westerville,_Ohio)_-_stained_glass,_arcade,_Saint_Andrew_Dung_Lac.jpg), photographer Nheyob, CC BY-SA 3.0. Image credits and license are shown in the card. The image depicts Andrew, not each companion martyr.
- Saint Martin of Tours: [Met Museum sculpture](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Saint_Martin_of_Tours_MET_DP144442.jpg), CC0.

Other saint entries still display the decorative figure, clearly avoiding any mistaken claim of an individual portrait. Existing explicitly assigned saint images (e.g. Josaphat) are preserved, including their image-source link. Source images come from Wikimedia Commons when online; offline artwork uses no remote network. Original prayer texts, liturgical dates, PDF/EPUB books, preferences and bookmarks are untouched.

Next expansion: add individually verified portraits with source and license documentation as time allows. Never automatically label an unsourced portrait as a specific saint.
