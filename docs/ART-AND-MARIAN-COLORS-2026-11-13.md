# Sacred art and Marian blue — v268

The art collection contains 37 works: 18 Eastern and 19 Western. The previous
ordinary-day Eastern pool was mostly three general-tagged works; the new deck
includes every work in the chosen tradition. Seasonal art leads on most seasonal
days, while Another traverses the full deck before repeating. Roman and
Traditional art retain their existing tradition and period boundaries.

Every record has an artist, creation date, origin and source link. Approximate
dates use “ca.”; catalogue date ranges remain ranges, and uncertain places are
qualified rather than inferred. The National Gallery's current Baptism dating
(probably about 1437–1445) replaces the older circa-1450 label; Rublev's Trinity
uses the Tretyakov Gallery's 1422–1427 dating. The generic Hodegetria and Dormition
entries are replaced by specific catalogued ivory icons. Twelve museum entries
pin an exact object image to its caption: ten from The Met, marked Public Domain,
and two from Cleveland, offered through its Open Access/CC0 programme. Source
URLs are recorded with each work in content.js; no museum description is copied
wholesale. Existing works retain the live verified-image lookup.

Captions use a title, artist line and aligned Created/Origin rows. Museum image
credits remain distinct from historical origin. Dates and origin remain readable
when an external image service is unavailable. External-media-blocked browser
checks exercise all 18 Eastern works, including a seasonal date, and narrow-phone
caption wrapping. They do not certify ongoing availability of external services.

Marian blue (#3b729f) is Ora's requested display colour for Marian observances.
It applies to Today badges, calendar dots and swatches, the colour legend and
shared page accents. Source vestment values and calendar precedence are retained.
Transferred Annunciation follows its observed date; an outranked or optional
Marian entry does not recolour the winning day. Joseph and other saints who only
mention the Virgin in their titles are excluded. Ukrainian and Melkite forms of
the December 9 feast are covered separately.
