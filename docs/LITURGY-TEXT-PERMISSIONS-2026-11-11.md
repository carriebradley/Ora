# Liturgy text permissions and source review

Reviewed 11 November 2026. Ora is a free, noncommercial, publicly hosted devotional website for the owner's personal prayer. This review records published terms and unresolved questions; it is not correspondence or a grant issued to Ora.

## Roman texts

ICEL's *Publication Policies*, section “Use of ICEL Materials on Global Computer Networks,” explicitly permits its approved and promulgated texts on a noncommercial website without obtaining separate oral or written permission, subject to conditions. These include free access, exact texts, appropriate ICEL notices at the beginning and end of the material, no implication of ICEL endorsement, and no extension of the permission to other publication forms. The permission may be modified or terminated.

Source: https://www.icelweb.org/PubPolicy.PDF

The policy was located in the search engine's indexed official PDF. Direct retrieval of the PDF and ICEL copyright page failed in this environment, so the complete current document and its revision date were not independently downloaded. Do not present this as an Ora-specific license or permission for material owned by another organization.

The official USCCB page below provides approved 2010 ICEL texts for selected fixed Mass parts: Kyrie, Gloria, Nicene Creed, Sanctus, Memorial Acclamations and Agnus Dei. It is not the entire Order of Mass, the Eucharistic Prayers, or the daily propers. Its musical-submission instructions concern music settings and do not by themselves grant a general app license.

https://www.usccb.org/committees/divine-worship/policies/mass-settings-texts

The USCCB's Order of Mass guide identifies the current Roman structure and distinguishes fixed parts from prayers and readings proper to the day:

https://www.usccb.org/prayer-and-worship/the-mass/order-of-mass

Ora now makes these resources available from Liturgy → Mass → Order of Mass · prayers and responses. This adds access to verified current texts without pretending the complete Missal has been imported.

The USCCB's new publication guidelines were approved 15 June 2025 and enter into force **29 November 2026**. They are not described here as already in force on the review date. Their scope includes digital distribution and devotional publications; they describe text authentication and other publication requirements. The devotional section treats private devotion separately from participation aids, but still describes review. Their precise interaction with ICEL's general noncommercial-website permission needs clarification for a public PWA with offline caching. Copyright permission and ecclesiastical publication approval are distinct questions.

https://www.usccb.org/committees/divine-worship/policies/guidelines-for-the-publication-of-liturgical-books
https://www.usccb.org/committees/divine-worship/policies/guidelines-for-the-publication-of-liturgical-books/devotional-and-other-non-liturgical-publications

The existing USCCB RSS allowance for daily readings does not itself license all Missal prayers or other Bible translations:

https://www.usccb.org/committees/divine-worship/policies/copyright-permissions-requirements

## Melkite texts

The supplied LITURGY2009.pdf is still linked by the Diocese of Newton. Its decree describes experimental approval through 31 October 2010. Continued hosting establishes where the diocese makes this edition available; it does not establish a later decree or a general right for another site to republish the translation. No general reuse grant or later confirming decree was found in the reviewed sources.

https://melkite.org/faith/faith-worship/new-english-translation-of-the-divine-liturgy
https://melkite.org/liturgical-resources

The official daily Gospel page currently exposes the unresolved shortcode `[sc_daily_readings]` in retrieved text. No dated, machine-readable Gospel feed was verified from that page.

https://melkite.org/daily-readings

## Concrete clarification questions

For ICEL/USCCB: Does ICEL's noncommercial-website grant cover a free publicly accessible devotional PWA, used by its owner for private prayer, displaying exact current ICEL Order of Mass and proper texts with the required notices and same-origin offline caching? Does the November 2026 publication framework require submission or authentication for this specific use, and which text sources and acknowledgments should be used? No commercial distribution, parish marketing, streaming or text-to-speech of these texts is proposed.

For Newton: Which English edition of the Divine Liturgy of St John Chrysostom is currently prescribed? May Ora reproduce that edition verbatim on its free devotional website with personal offline caching, and what attribution, approval or license is required? If the linked 2009 draft remains prescribed, is there a later decree confirming it?

No inquiry has been sent to any organization, and no affirmative answer has been invented. Ora remains public. Full Mass and Melkite liturgy imports remain incomplete while these questions are unresolved; official source access and the verified dated reading references remain available.
